What it does

Built for the way painting work actually runs.

Paint is unforgiving about vague paperwork: once it is on the wall, the argument is expensive. Crewkat makes the agreement specific — each room a line, each color and finish named and approved, changes signed as change orders — so the work itself is the only thing left to think about.

  • Room-by-room scope. Build the estimate the way clients think: living room, primary bedroom, cabinets, exterior — each its own line with prep, coats, and finish spelled out, so “was that included?” has a written answer.
  • Color and finish approvals, in writing. Put the color and finish choices on the estimate and get them approved through the client's link before you buy paint. The approved selection lives on the job — not in a text thread under a photo of a swatch.
  • Options clients can tick. The extra bathroom, the ceiling upgrade, the cabinet refinishing — priced as optional add-ons the client accepts or declines on the estimate itself, with the total updating as they choose.
  • Contracts and change orders that keep up. Get the agreement signed from a link, and when scope grows mid-job — it always does — send a change order and get it signed before the roller comes out, not after.
  • Photos that sell the next job. Before and after shots per room, filed to the job as you go. Pair them into proof packs for the client, and your job record becomes your portfolio.
  • Deposits and balances, tracked. Invoice the deposit at signing and the balance at the final walk. With Pay Now on Premium, clients pay by card or bank transfer from the invoice link; every payment shows against the job.

How it works

Three steps, from first call to final payment.

1

Scope it room by room

Walk the home, list every room and surface as its own line, name the colors and finishes, and price the extras as options. The client approves the exact scope in writing.

2

Run the job on the record

Daily logs and photos per room as prep and paint progress. Extra work becomes a signed change order before it becomes a disagreement.

3

Walk it, invoice it, done

Final walk with the client, touch-ups noted on the job, and the balance invoice sent — payable from its link while the last drop cloth is still being folded.

No more “wrong color” calls

The approval is the answer.

“That is not the color I picked” ends when the picked color is written on the approved estimate with its finish, next to the room it belongs to. Crewkat keeps that approval on the job through the final walk, so memory never gets a vote — and your crew paints from the record, not from a screenshot of a screenshot.

Common questions

Painting, answered.

How do color approvals work?

Colors and finishes go on the estimate as part of the scope, and the client approves them through their document link before work starts. The approved choices stay on the job record for the crew and for the final walk.

Can I price a job room by room?

Yes — that is the natural way to build an estimate in Crewkat. Each room or surface is its own line, and extras like cabinets or ceilings can be optional add-ons the client ticks and approves.

How do deposits and final payments work?

Invoice the deposit when the contract is signed and the balance at completion, all against the same job. With Premium, each invoice link carries Pay Now for card or bank transfer payments.
