What it does

Built for the way handyman work actually runs.

Volume is the whole game in handyman work: more stops, less friction per stop. Crewkat is built for many small jobs — open one in seconds, price it in minutes, work the list, photograph the result, and send the invoice from the driveway. The day ends with the paperwork already done.

  • Fast estimates, honestly fast. Small jobs get small paperwork: a few lines, a clear total, sent from your phone. Bigger lists get options on the estimate that the client ticks — do it all, or start with the top five.
  • Punch lists that close out. Build the list with the client on the walk-through, then check items off as they are done — each with a photo when it matters. The finished list is the record the invoice matches.
  • Many jobs, one clear day. Every stop is a job with its client, address, and list — today, tomorrow, and the call-back next month all in one place instead of a text thread per house.
  • Before and after, every time. Thirty seconds of photos per task — the door that stuck, the drywall patch, the new fixture — protects you on callbacks and quietly builds your portfolio.
  • Same-day invoicing, paid from the link. Convert the finished list to an invoice before you leave. With Pay Now on Premium, clients pay by card or bank transfer from the link — no “the check is in the mail” for a $300 job.
  • Client history that compounds. Every visit to a house adds to its record — past lists, photos, invoices — so repeat clients, property managers, and landlords get a pro who remembers everything.

How it works

Three steps, from first call to final payment.

1

Walk it and list it

Open the job at the door, walk the house with the client, and build the punch list together. Price it as one estimate with options for the maybes.

2

Work the list

Check items off as they are done, photograph the fixes, and add anything new as a line the client approves — no scope creep in either direction.

3

Invoice in the driveway

Finished list becomes the invoice in a tap. Paid from the link or recorded on the spot, the stop is fully closed before the truck moves.

The list is the contract

Agreed, checked, photographed.

Handyman friction is rarely about skill — it is about the list: what was promised, what was added, what “done” means. A written punch list the client approved, checked off with photos, answers all three. And when a landlord sends the same list for the next unit, duplicate the job and go — the paperwork is already written.

Common questions

Handyman, answered.

Is Crewkat worth it for jobs that only take an hour?

That is the point of it. Opening a job, pricing it, and invoicing it take a few minutes total, and you get a proper record, photos, and a paid-from-the-link invoice for every stop.

Can a client approve just part of a punch list?

Yes. Build the full list as an estimate with optional items, and the client ticks what they want done now. The approved items set the price, and the rest stays on the record for next time.

How do I get paid the same day?

Send the invoice from the driveway — with Premium it carries Pay Now, so the client pays by card or bank transfer from the link, and the payment records itself against the job.
